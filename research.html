M.S. thesis: dengue and weather in Bangladesh

Modeling and Predicting Daily Dengue Cases in Bangladesh Using Meteorological Factors: A Comparative Study of Statistical and Machine-Learning Approaches

Department of Statistics, University of Chittagong · Supervisor: Mohammad Salim Zahangir, PhD, Professor · Submitted September 2026 · Unpublished, not peer reviewed.

DengueDisease surveillanceNegative Binomial regressionSeven-day forecastingLog-RidgeXGBoostSHAPTemporal validation

Background and objectives

Dengue is a climate-sensitive public-health problem in Bangladesh. Explaining how weather relates to reported cases and forecasting short-term burden are different analytical tasks. The thesis examines meteorological associations with daily reported counts and compares seven-day-ahead forecasting approaches across all eight administrative divisions.

Data and design

A retrospective, two-track ecological spatio-temporal design.

Track A: explanation

Daily dengue counts from the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS) linked with temperature, rainfall, relative humidity and sunshine duration from the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), 2022–2024: 8,768 division-days.

Track B: forecasting

A separate leakage-controlled dataset of 10,904 origin–target records, limited to information available at forecast origin, with weather from a distinct reanalysis-based series. 8,472 records were used for chronological development and 2,432 for the 2025 temporal test.

Methods

Statistical models

  • Poisson and Negative Binomial regression compared; Negative Binomial type 2 (NB2) as the primary model
  • Prespecified lag comparison and a nonlinear temperature term
  • Sensitivity analyses for serial dependence (AR(1) GEE; PPML) and excess zeros (zero-inflated NB)

Forecasting and validation

  • Persistence baseline, Log-Ridge, Random Forest and XGBoost
  • Rolling-origin validation and a final temporal test, January–October 2025
  • SHAP and feature ablation for interpretation

Main findings

  • Of 483,715 cases reported in 2022–2024, 320,644 occurred in 2023; Dhaka accounted for 55.71%.
  • Counts were severely overdispersed (Poisson Pearson dispersion about 30), favouring NB2.
  • A 28-day lag minimised AIC, though a 14-day lag fitted almost as well.
  • Temperature showed the most robust adjusted association, nonlinear and delayed. Rainfall and sunshine showed no robust independent linear association; humidity was sensitive to the dependence structure.
  • In the 2025 test, Log-Ridge had the lowest division-day mean absolute error (8.854), ahead of persistence (9.451), XGBoost (9.792) and Random Forest (11.144).
  • Recent dengue history dominated predictive information: adding history to weather and structural features cut development MAE by 71.47%, whereas adding meteorology to history and structural features cut it by 8.22%.
  • Error rose sharply at epidemic peaks: MAE 84.86 and bias −83.34 at or above the 95th-percentile burden (the models under-predicted peaks).

Explanatory association and predictive utility are complementary but distinct. Short-horizon forecasting should prioritise timely surveillance history, keep meteorology as supplementary context, and monitor peak-burden error and uncertainty.

Limitations and future research

The findings are ecological, rest on passively reported surveillance counts and three annual cycles dominated by the 2023 epidemic, and do not establish causality. Prospective validation is required before operational use. Division-level analysis cannot address urban-scale processes.

It offers a statistical baseline for finer-scale studies of climate, urban environmental conditions and disease risk, which need population denominators and land-use, water-body, mobility and entomological data.
